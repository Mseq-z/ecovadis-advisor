# EcoVadis — Sustainable Procurement Theme Reference

## Activated Criteria (typical)
- Supplier environmental practices
- Supplier social practices (L&HR in supply chain)
- Supplier screening / due diligence
- Supplier development and capacity building
- Sustainable product / service design (if applicable)
- Responsible purchasing practices

## Key Evidence Documents

### Supplier Code of Conduct (SCoC) — Required Elements
- Covers: environment, labour/human rights, ethics, anti-corruption
- References: ILO, UN Global Compact, applicable laws
- Describes: right to audit clause
- Sanctions for non-compliance
- Applies to: all direct suppliers, extended to sub-suppliers ideally
- Signed/endorsed by senior management

### Responsible Sourcing Policy — Required Elements
- Commitment to considering ESG performance in supplier selection
- Integration of sustainability in procurement decisions
- Conflict minerals / high-risk material sourcing (if applicable)
- Reference to supplier screening process

### Supplier Sustainability Questionnaire (SSQ) — Key Sections
```
Section 1: Company Information
Section 2: Environmental Management
  - Environmental policy in place? (Y/N + upload link)
  - GHG emissions tracked? (Y/N + data)
  - ISO 14001 or equivalent?
Section 3: Labour & Human Rights
  - H&S policy in place?
  - Incident rate data
  - Child/forced labour policy?
  - ISO 45001 / SA8000?
Section 4: Ethics
  - Anti-corruption policy?
  - Whistleblower channel?
  - CoC acknowledged by employees?
Section 5: Certifications and Scores
  - EcoVadis score (if rated)
  - Other sustainability certifications
Section 6: Improvement Commitments
```

### Supplier Risk Assessment Matrix
EcoVadis rewards structured supplier segmentation:

| Risk Tier | Criteria | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | High spend, high ESG risk country/sector | Full SSQ + on-site audit |
| High | High spend OR high ESG risk | Full SSQ + remote audit |
| Medium | Moderate spend/risk | Abbreviated SSQ |
| Low | Low spend, low risk | Self-declaration or CoC sign-off |

### Actions Evidence
- Supplier mapping / spend analysis (showing # of suppliers covered by CoC)
- Signed supplier CoC acknowledgements or procurement contract clauses
- Completed supplier SSQs from top suppliers
- Supplier audit reports (internal or third-party)
- Supplier sustainability training or capacity-building records
- Corrective Action Plans issued to suppliers

### High-Value Signals for Procurement
- **CSDDD-aligned due diligence process** — EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive
- **Sedex / SMETA membership** — Social audit framework
- **CDP Supply Chain programme participation**
- **Pilot / development supplier programme** — Shows supplier capacity building not just assessment

## Quick Wins for Score 40–50 → 60+
1. Publish a Supplier Code of Conduct (if not already done — this alone can add 5–8 points)
2. Send SSQ to top 10 suppliers by spend — even a 50% response rate counts as evidence
3. Add a "right to audit" clause to standard procurement contracts
4. Document your supplier onboarding process to show ESG questions are asked
5. Map your supplier base by risk tier — even a rough version shows programme maturity

## Note for Tech/SaaS Companies
Procurement weight may be lower (10–15%) for pure software companies. Prioritise:
- Cloud/infrastructure suppliers (e.g., AWS, Azure, GCP) — ask for their sustainability reports
- Key software vendors — collect CoC acknowledgements
- Staffing / contingent labour suppliers — ensure L&HR standards flow down
